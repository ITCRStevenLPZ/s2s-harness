    ☑ Final review

    North Star — what the project actually wants

    Your primary goal is a spontaneous, natural, aesthetically pleasing conversation with both Model A and Model B. It should feel like chatting with a friend or an acquaintance — not like querying an assistant.

    • Adopt the persona. Actively assume the assigned role and step into the situation. Use natural transitions ("that's a fair point, but what if…") instead of firing question after question. Let yourself go a little and actually converse.
    • Keep 1:1 consistency. Both models must be evaluated under identical conditions: same energy, same depth, same topic, same number of turns. If you spend 4 turns on vintage watches with Model A, do the same with Model B.
    • Turns. Minimum 4 turns per model. Extend beyond that when you need more evidence for a fair comparison.
    • Stay in character. Never discuss the prompt or the task itself while interacting with the models.
    Language note. Instructions and your written justification are in English. Your spoken conversation with the voice model happens in whichever language you are authorized to work in (Spanish, French, Chinese, etc.).
    Turn-count reconciliation. Project training material mentions both "2–6 turns" and "minimum 4 turns". Treat 4 as the hard floor and 4–6 as the working target; go longer whenever the comparison is not yet decidable.

    Audio position check

    Listen to the full clip and select Left (louder in the left ear), Center (equal in both), or Right (louder in the right). The difference is subtle and audio is still present in both ears.

    Testing adaptability via interruptions

    Interrupt mid-response by changing your mind, adding a constraint, or redirecting. Observe whether the model acknowledges the interruption, adapts appropriately, and remains coherent.

    Skip (technical issue)

    Use Skip only when the model freezes, gives no response, or the connection fails. Include a brief description. Skip is never for poor performance.

    Rating hierarchy & the four dimensions
    Naturalness / Engagement  >  Utility  >  Audio Quality

    Utility and Audio Quality carry less weight, but both must still clear a basic satisfaction threshold: no misinformation, no hallucination, no jarring audio errors.

    DimensionThe question you are answering
    Naturalness / Engagement / AestheticsWhich model sounded more human, expressive and warm? Score robotic intonation and unnatural rhythm lower.
    UtilityWhich response was genuinely more useful, practical and grounded in real facts?
    Audio QualityWhich model delivered greater clarity, without distortion, clicks, pops or noticeable cutoffs?
    Overall PreferenceAll things considered, which conversation would you rather continue — A or B?

    Task success: Pass / Partial / Fail, recorded per model.

    Audio Quality tolerance. Small imperfections are acceptable. Do not penalize a model for light background noise that sounds like the audio was "recorded in a normal room/office" (slight room impulse), or for a faint "air" sound in the recording — as long as it is not jarring or annoying. When relevant, state in your justification whether you penalized Audio Quality or not.
    Compare both models └─ Baseline met? ├─ Misinformation / major failure ─→ do NOT prefer that model └─ Baseline OK ─→ Naturalness & Engagement ─→ Utility ─→ Audio Quality ─→ Submit Vote
    Error clusters you must report
    • Repetitive words or phrases across turns.
    • Responses degrading noticeably as the conversation continues.
    • The model interrupting you, or ignoring your attempts to interrupt it.
    • Awkward silences (failing to respond).
    • References to embodied experiences — a digital AI offering to do something that requires a physical body, such as grabbing a cup of coffee, knocking on a door, or handling objects.
    • Speech recognition failures.
    • Refusal to follow instructions.
    Important nuance. Even with one of these errors present, a clip that sounds very natural by the project's North Star can still be selected as the better one. If you do that, you must explain it clearly and with evidence in your justification.
    Critical mistakes to avoid

    🚫 AI language

    Do not speak to the model like a generic "helpful assistant". Avoid prompts that start with "As an AI…" — they destroy the flow and the aesthetics of the conversation.

    🚫 Mis-rating Audio Quality

    Do not choose "Both Good" for Audio Quality if one model has noticeable background noise, clicks, cutoffs or similar problems that are clearly bothersome. Listen to each model's audio in isolation on stereo headphones to catch these.

    🚫 Contradictory or missing justifications

    Every task requires a detailed, accurate justification. If you decide Model A was better overall, your dimension selections and your written text must align logically with that choice. Name the exact moment where the problem occurs and describe each model's specific behaviors whenever possible.

    🚫 Misreading the scenario context

    Pay attention to what the scenario asks for. In a Hallucination Fishing / Redteam scenario — where you deliberately state false information and the model is supposed to catch it — a model that successfully refuses or disagrees with the false facts is a success signal, not a failure.

    There is no separate guide explaining each scenario, because there are endless cases. Every scenario is self-explanatory: reading the one assigned to your task is all you need.
    How to write a strong justification

    A strong justification does more than say which clip "sounds better". It explains the choice using the project's rating hierarchy.

    1. Clear preference — state which clip/interaction is better overall.
    2. Hierarchy reference — explain whether the decision rests on naturalness, utility or audio quality.
    3. Specific evidence — cite observable details from both clips. Always provide concrete examples from wherever the evidence originates.
    4. Threshold rules — watch for hallucinations, false information, cutoffs, distortion, annoying artifacts or major instruction-following problems. Naturalness is the most important dimension, but every clip must clear the minimum threshold before naturalness can carry its full weight. If there are threshold problems, naturalness can be displaced by those errors when they are considerable.
    5. Error clusters — if present, name the problem explicitly: interruption, failed correction, repetitive phrasing, embodied-experience claims.

    Template

    "I prefer Clip A overall because ___. Under the project hierarchy, ___ matters most here because ___. Clip A showed this through ___, while Clip B was weaker because ___. I also did / did not observe any threshold-level issues such as hallucination, audio cutoff, distortion, or failed instruction-following. Therefore, Clip A is the stronger fit for the project's North Star."
    Worked examples (4)
    Example 1 — Naturalness vs. over-structured Utility

    Context: the user asks why a common houseplant can make an apartment feel cozier.

    Correct answer: Clip A
    I prefer Clip A overall because it better matches the project's North Star of spontaneous, natural, aesthetically pleasing conversation. Under the rating hierarchy, Naturalness / Engagement matters most here because both clips are understandable and useful enough, and neither has a major audio issue. Clip A feels warm and conversational, with specific imagery like noticing a new leaf and moving the plant closer to the window. Clip B is useful, but it sounds more like a structured assistant response than a natural voice conversation. Therefore, Clip A is the stronger overall fit.
    Example 2 — Abrupt audio cutoff

    Context: the user asks for advice on relaxing before an intense workday. Audio note: Clip B cuts off abruptly at the end and has a small pop.

    Correct answer: Clip A
    I prefer Clip A overall because Clip B has a clear audio quality issue that crosses the project's threshold. Clip B cuts off mid-word at the end and includes an audible pop, so it should not be treated as "Both Good" for audio quality. While Clip B is still somewhat useful, the cutoff makes the response feel incomplete and objectively weaker. Clip A gives a complete, calm, natural response with no obvious audio artifacts. Because audio cutoffs and jarring artifacts must be flagged, Clip A is the better choice.
    Example 3 — Benign false premise

    Context: the user asks "why do owls sleep upside down like bats?"

    Correct answer: Clip B
    I prefer Clip B overall because Clip A fails the utility threshold by accepting a false premise and inventing an explanation. The project prioritizes naturalness and engagement, but factual accuracy still has to meet a basic threshold. Clip A sounds fluent, but it incorrectly claims that owls sleep upside down like bats. Clip B is still conversational while appropriately correcting the premise and giving the accurate alternative. Because hallucinations and false-premise acceptance are objective issues, Clip B is stronger.
    Example 4 — Error clusters must be reported

    Context: the user asks for help drafting a polite message to a neighbor about noise. Behavior note: Clip A also keeps talking briefly when the user tries to interrupt.

    Correct answer: both choices are defensible, but Clip A's errors must be flagged

    Choosing Clip A:

    I prefer Clip A overall because it is more natural, polite, and useful for the neighbor-message scenario. Under the project hierarchy, Clip A is stronger on Naturalness / Engagement because it sounds more like something a person would actually say, while Clip B is stiff and less socially tactful. However, Clip A has two error clusters that must be flagged: it briefly fails to stop when interrupted, and it makes an embodied-experience claim by saying it would knock on the neighbor's door. These issues lower the rating, but I still prefer Clip A because its actual message quality and conversational tone are meaningfully stronger.

    Choosing Clip B:

    I prefer Clip B overall because Clip A contains objective error clusters that affect the interaction quality. Clip A is warmer and more natural, but it talks over the user during an attempted interruption and makes an embodied-experience claim about knocking on the neighbor's door. Those issues should be explicitly flagged. Clip B is less engaging, but it avoids those interaction errors and still provides a usable message. Because the errors in Clip A are concrete and observable, Clip B can be defended as the safer overall choice.
    Memory hooks — self-quiz
    • What audio hardware is required, and what is banned? Phase-aligned stereo headphones are required; speakers and mono earbuds are both prohibited.
    • What are the three audio-position options? Left (louder in left ear), Center (equal in both), Right (louder in right ear) — and the difference is subtle.
    • What is the preference ranking order? Naturalness & Engagement, then Utility, then Audio Quality.
    • What is the prompt consistency rule? Both models must get prompts of the same level of specificity — giving one model a more detailed prompt is a disqualifying error.
    • What three behaviors do you watch for when you interrupt? Whether it acknowledges the interruption, adapts appropriately, and remains coherent.
    • When do you use Skip? Only for technical failures — freeze, no response, connection issues — never for poor performance, and always with a brief description.
    • What is the turn count? Minimum 4 per model, 4–6 as the working target; go longer when you need more evidence.
    • Name four flaggable issues. Repetitive language, degradation across turns, interrupting the user, awkward silence, ignoring interruptions or corrections, speech recognition failures, impossible real-world (embodied) references, refusal to follow instructions.
    • What are the four evaluation categories? Overall preference, utility, naturalness & engagement, audio quality — plus task success (pass / partial / fail).
    • When must a very natural model NOT be preferred? When it has obvious misinformation, severe audio issues, or major failures — utility and audio quality still have to meet a baseline.
    • What four questions do you ask before submitting? Which conversation would I rather continue; did I evaluate the entire interaction; did either model noticeably break down; can I clearly explain my preference.
